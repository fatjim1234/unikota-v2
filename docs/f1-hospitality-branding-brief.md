## Pre-brief (per instructions, before the full deliverables)

### 1. Right brand direction — 5 bullets

- Lead with **operating age and scale**, not product variety. "1983" and
  "3,300+ outlets" do more procurement work in the first five seconds than
  any brand story — a buyer is first screening for *will this vendor still
  exist and perform mid-contract*, not for personality.
- Position hospitality as **one served vertical inside an existing supply
  operation**, never as a new pitch. The credibility comes from "we already
  do this at scale for others," not "we would like to serve this market."
- Keep the tone **flat and factual**, deliberately under-selling. In
  procurement evaluation, restraint reads as competence; enthusiasm reads
  as compensation for a gap.
- Separate **what is confirmed today** from **what would be scoped for this
  engagement** — every current page already does this well (OEM copy
  explicitly avoids unconfirmed claims); extend that discipline to the new
  hospitality material rather than relaxing it for a bigger opportunity.
- Make **customisation and export coordination** the throughline connecting
  hospitality to everything else Unikota already does — an F1-adjacent
  hospitality buyer needs branded, event-specific, deadline-driven,
  cross-border-capable supply, which is a description of the OEM/export
  business Unikota already runs, not a new capability being invented for
  this opportunity.

`Hospitality & Event Supply` sits as its own top-level nav item, not a

### 3. Recommended company profile narrative flow

Identity and standing → what is manufactured and supplied → market reach
and scale proof → hospitality/event-specific relevance → OEM and
customisation process → export and delivery coordination → commercial
next step. Legal and contact information appears twice — once near the
front for immediate verification, once on the closing page as the acted-on
next step. No page exists purely for tone; every page carries a specific
buyer question.

## A. Brand diagnosis

Unikota should be positioned to an F1 hospitality buyer as a **forty-year
Malaysian tissue and hygiene manufacturer with an existing branded and
private-label supply operation**, of which event and hospitality-grade
customised supply is a proven, not aspirational, capability. The
opportunity is not to invent a hospitality brand story — it is to surface
the OEM, customisation and export coordination work Unikota already does
for corporate and retail clients, and present it in the register a
procurement evaluator expects: specific, restrained, currently operating,
and backed by scale and longevity a buyer can verify.

## B. Website strategy

**Sitemap**

```
/                                Home
/about                           Company
/products                        Brands & Products
/products/[brand]                Per-brand pages (existing)
/hospitality                     Hospitality & Event Supply (new)
/oem                              OEM, Customisation & Export
/contact                         Contact / Request a Proposal
```

`/manufacturing`, `/export` and `/solutions` fold into `/oem` and
`/hospitality` as sections rather than standing alone — a procurement
reviewer under time pressure should not have to visit five pages to
assemble one picture of capability.

**Page hierarchy inside each page:** identity/credibility line first,
then the specific relevant capability, then proof, then a single
unambiguous next action. No page should require scrolling past decoration
to reach the first fact.

**Above the fold, homepage:** company name and registration standing
(small, permanent, not hero-sized), one sentence stating what Unikota
manufactures and for whom, and the scale proof line (1983 / 3,300+ outlets
/ 9 export markets) — all visible without scrolling, on desktop and
mobile.

**Sections that matter most for buyer trust, in order of weight:**
1. Legal/registration consistency (footer, contact page, PDF — identical
   everywhere)
2. Scale proof stated in specific numbers, not ranges
3. A named, current OEM/customisation process with no invented steps
4. Export market list actually present on the export-facing page (a real
   gap today — see audit)
5. A hospitality-specific page that does not read as a copy-paste of the
   OEM page

## F. Red-flag corrections — before this goes to an F1-related buyer

1. **Brand count doesn't match the claim.** The verified fact sheet says
   seven brands. The live homepage and about page copy name only four
   (Cili, Wasabi, Unisoft, Aimishu) — BabyLike, SUMO and Cili Padi exist on
   the site's brand pages but are absent from the narrative copy. A buyer
   who reads the homepage claim and then browses `/products` will count
   six or seven brands, not four — an easy, avoidable inconsistency.
2. **No hospitality-specific page exists.** Hospitality relevance is
   currently implied through one bullet inside a homepage "buyers" list
   and a general OEM "project types" mention. For a hospitality-sector
   buyer specifically, that reads as incidental rather than served.
3. **The export page has no market list.** `/export`'s content currently
   ships with an empty items list — the nine-market claim lives only on
   the homepage. The page whose entire purpose is export credibility
   should not be the one page missing the actual market list.
4. **Vague quantifiers where the brief mandates specific numbers.** Some
   existing copy uses "thousands of outlets" where the verified,
   specific figure — 3,300+ — is available and should be used instead.
   Procurement reviewers read a specific number as audited; a rounded
   phrase as unverified.
5. **No placeholder or dead-route risk to close out first.** The
   underlying site has a small number of routes (cart, shop,
   subscriptions) that are not part of the current commerce-free B2B
   strategy. Confirm these are not linked from primary navigation and are
   either completed or removed before external review — an exposed
   half-built page is a more damaging trust signal than a missing one.
6. **What is already correct and should not be touched:** legal name,
   registration number, incorporation date, and registered address are
   identical across the settings content, the about page and the site
   footer today. This is a genuine strength worth preserving exactly as
   is, not a gap to fix.
